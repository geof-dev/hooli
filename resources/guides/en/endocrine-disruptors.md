## What is an endocrine disruptor?

Your endocrine system is the set of glands that make hormones: the thyroid, the ovaries, the adrenal glands and others. Hormones are chemical messengers. In tiny amounts, they steer growth, metabolism, the menstrual cycle, fertility, mood and sleep.

The World Health Organization defines an endocrine disruptor as a substance, or a mixture of substances, that changes how this system works and causes harm as a result. Some imitate a hormone, like oestrogen. Some block one. Others change how much of a hormone the body makes, or how quickly it's broken down.

Since 2023, EU chemical rules recognise endocrine disruption as a hazard of its own, for human health and for the environment, next to hazards like toxicity or causing cancer.

## Why it matters, and when it matters most

Hormones act in very small amounts, so a substance that interferes with them can matter even in small doses. Three things make the difference:

- **Timing.** Pregnancy, early childhood and puberty are the most sensitive windows, because hormones are shaping organs that are still developing.
- **Repetition.** Exposure adds up across products and across days: a lotion, a shampoo, a takeaway lunch, a receipt.
- **Mixtures.** We're rarely exposed to one substance at a time, and their effects can add up.

Research has linked some endocrine disruptors to fertility problems, thyroid disorders, early puberty and some hormone-related conditions. For many substances the evidence is still being gathered. That's why agencies keep reassessing them, and why it's reasonable to lower your exposure where it's easy, without panic.

## Where they're found every day

| Where | Substances worth knowing | What they're there for |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetics and personal care | Some parabens (propylparaben, butylparaben), some UV filters (benzophenone-3, homosalate), triclosan | Preserving, protecting from the sun, killing bacteria |
| Fragrance | Diethyl phthalate | Making scent last. Fragrance is listed only as "parfum" |
| Food packaging and kitchenware | Bisphenols, phthalates, PFAS | Hard plastics, can linings, grease-proof paper, non-stick coatings |
| Till receipts | Bisphenols | Thermal paper |
| Home | Some flame retardants, PFAS | Furniture foam, electronics, stain-proof and waterproof treatments |

For the names to look for on a cosmetic label, and how to read one, see our guide to [cosmetic ingredients that may affect hormones](/guides/cosmetic-ingredients-hormones).

## What the rules already do

EU rules on chemicals are among the strictest in the world, and they keep tightening:

- **Cosmetics.** Five parabens have been banned since 2014. Propylparaben and butylparaben are capped, and banned in leave-on products made for the nappy area of children under three. Since 2022, benzophenone-3 and homosalate are allowed only in lower amounts.
- **Food contact.** The EU has banned bisphenol A in materials that touch food, such as can linings and reusable bottles. The ban was adopted in 2024, and most products had to comply by July 2026.
- **Receipts.** Bisphenol A has been restricted in thermal paper since 2020.
- **Drinking water.** EU rules now set limits for bisphenol A and PFAS in tap water.
- **France went further.** Since 1 January 2026, cosmetics containing PFAS can no longer be made or sold there.

Allowed doesn't always mean ideal. Limits are set for the general population; during pregnancy, or for a baby's products, choosing to avoid some ingredients is a reasonable precaution.

## Simple ways to lower your exposure

You don't need to change everything at once. Start where exposure is highest: products you use every day, that stay on your skin, or that touch warm food.

1. **Heat food in glass or ceramic**, not plastic. Heat helps chemicals move from plastic into food. Keep plastic containers for cold things.
2. **Choose fragrance-free** for products that stay on your skin, like body lotion. "Parfum" can stand for dozens of ingredients.
3. **Read leave-on products first**: creams, lotions, make-up and sunscreen. Shower gel and shampoo spend much less time on your skin.
4. **Air your home** for ten minutes a day, even in winter, and dust with a damp cloth. Many chemicals from furniture and electronics settle in household dust.
5. **Skip the receipt** when you can, and don't give receipts to children to play with.
6. **Replace scratched non-stick pans**, and consider stainless steel or cast iron when you buy new ones.
7. **Cook fresh food more often** than canned or heavily processed food, which spends longer in contact with packaging.

Replace things as they run out. Throwing everything away at once costs a lot and doesn't change much more.

## When to talk to a health professional

If you live with a thyroid condition, endometriosis, PCOS or a meningioma, or you're planning a pregnancy, it's worth raising these questions with your doctor or midwife. They know your history and can tell you what matters most for you.

Some medicines act on hormones too. Never stop a prescribed treatment on your own: bring your questions to your doctor. Our guide to [progestins and meningioma](/guides/progestins-meningioma) lists the questions worth asking.
